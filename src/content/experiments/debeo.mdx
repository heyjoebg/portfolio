<div class="single-col mb-50">
  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>Debeo, a private expense tracker</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50">Debeo is a small, private expense tracker I'm building for my brother and me to replace a messy Excel workflow. It's my playground for <strong>vibe-coding</strong>, rapid prototyping, and exploring what an <strong>AI-enabled product designer</strong> can ship on nights/weekends—without waiting for a "perfect" work case study.</p>

  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>Why I built it</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50">We manage shared + personal expenses with an Excel sheet that "works"… until it doesn't:<br /><br />- Too many manual steps (copy/paste, categories, splitting, balances).<br />- The mobile experience is not good for a table based product and 80% of the time we log expenses on mobile and not on desktop.<br />- Easy to break formulas / lose consistency over time.<br />- Hard to answer simple questions fast: <em>"How much did we spend on X last month?"</em></p>

  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>Users</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50"><strong>- Me + my brother</strong> (busy, not accounting-obsessed, want clarity without friction).<br />- Future-friendly by design (so it could scale to more people later), but right now it's intentionally <strong>built for our needs first</strong>.</p>

  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>What success looks like</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50"><strong>- We stop using the spreadsheet for day-to-day tracking.<br />- Logging an expense takes {'<'}10 seconds.<br />- Monthly totals, category breakdowns, and "who owes who" are always correct.</strong></p>

  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>Constraints</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50"><strong>- Private product (not open-source, not public-facing).<br />- Must work well on mobile first (where expenses happen).<br />- Needs to support "real life" cases: split expenses, recurring bills, reimbursements, multi-currency (USD/COP).</strong></p>
</div>

<Highlight text={'I\'m not doing "perfect Figma first."'} />

<div class="single-col mb-50">
  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>My approach (how I'm building it)</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50"><strong>This is intentionally an <em>experiment</em> in modern product-making:<br />- Vibe-coded prototyping: I iterate quickly from idea → working flow → refine<br />- AI as a teammate: fast scaffolding, edge-case mapping, UI copy variants, validation rules, and data modeling drafts<br />- Design + build loop: I'm not doing "perfect Figma first." I'm using design thinking while shipping the experience in code, tightening the UX as I go.<br />- AI helps a ton, but only when I'm clear about the job-to-be-done and constraints.</strong></p>

  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>Core flows</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50"><strong>1. Quick add expense (amount → category → notes → done).<br />2. Split logic (mine / his / shared) + settlement tracking.<br />3. Monthly view with totals and category rollups.<br />4. Simple insights (top categories, trends, "where did the money go?").</strong></p>

  <h2 class="case-study-subtitle"><strong>Design principles</strong></h2>
  <p class="heyjoe_paragraph mb-50"><strong>- Fast by default: reduce typing, smart defaults.<br />- Low shame UX: friendly, lightweight, keeps you moving.<br />- Clarity {'>'} complexity: don't turn it into accounting software.</strong></p>
</div>
